# Welcome to Drydock

Welcome! Drydock is a friendly 3D modeling program for designing buildings, rooms, furniture and anything else you can picture. You do not need any special equipment to begin: a mouse, a keyboard and a little patience are enough to make your first model.

## What Drydock is

Drydock is a general-purpose 3D modeling program. Many people use it for architecture: houses, rooms and the things inside them. Others design furniture, products or tools. A design is built from simple pieces: edges and faces. An edge is a straight line between two points, and a face is the flat surface that those edges enclose. You start by drawing flat shapes on the ground or on an existing face, and then you pull those shapes into solids. A flat rectangle becomes a box, and a flat circle becomes a cylinder. Because every solid is made from these basic pieces, you can keep changing a design as your idea develops.

Working this way feels a lot like sketching on paper, except that your sketch has depth. You can add a wall where you need one, cut an opening where you want a window, and check the result from any angle as you go. The important thing to remember is that you always begin flat and then give the flat shape some thickness. Once you know how to draw a shape and pull it, you already know the heart of the program.

3D printing is one use Drydock supports especially well. When you want a physical copy, the [3D printing](printing.md) page shows how to check a model and send it to a printer.

## Where to start

Read the pages below in order when you are new. Each one is short and builds on the one before it.

- Start with [The Drydock window](window.md) to learn where the menus, toolbars and panels live.
- Next, read [Moving around](navigation.md) to orbit, pan and zoom with confidence.
- Then try [Selecting](selecting.md) so you can pick up the right edge or face every time.
- After that, open [Drawing](drawing.md) to make lines, rectangles, circles and arcs.
- Then read [Extrude](extrude.md) to turn those flat shapes into solids.
- [Moving, rotating and scaling](modify.md) changes the things you have selected.
- [Measuring and units](measuring.md) covers distances and the model's units.
- [Groups and components](groups.md) keeps pieces together and lets you reuse them.
- [Materials and painting](materials.md) adds color and texture.
- [Files, import and export](files.md) saves your work and moves models in and out.
- [3D printing](printing.md) checks a model and prepares it for a printer.
- [Keyboard shortcuts](shortcuts.md) lists the keys for every tool.
- [Drydock on Linux](linux.md) explains the desktop entry and icons it creates.

Take your time, try each step in the program as you read, and do not worry about making mistakes. Undo is always close by, and every master began with a wobbly first rectangle. Have fun building something that is truly yours.
