# Moving around

## Orbit

Use the Orbit tool (key `O`) to turn the view around the model. You can also hold the middle mouse button and drag to orbit without changing tools. Orbiting changes only your point of view, never the model itself. Hold `Shift` while orbiting to pan instead.

## Pan

Pan slides the view sideways and up or down without turning it. Pick the Pan tool with key `H`, then drag. You can also hold `Shift` while you orbit. Panning is handy when you want to see a part of the model that has moved off the screen without changing the angle you are looking from.

## Zoom

The scroll wheel zooms toward the cursor, so the place you point at stays in view. The Zoom tool (key `Z`) zooms by dragging up and down. Press `Shift+Z`, or choose View > Zoom Extents, to fit the whole model in the window. Zoom does not move the model in space; it only changes how close the camera appears to be.

## Standard views

The View menu has named views that look straight at the model from a fixed direction. View > Iso is `F2`, View > Top is `F3`, View > Front is `F4`, View > Right is `F5`, View > Back is `F6`, View > Left is `F7` and View > Bottom is `F8`. A standard view is a quick way to check that parts line up, because the same side is always shown the same way.

## Perspective and parallel

`Shift+P` switches between perspective and parallel projection, and the View menu has View > Switch to Parallel Projection and View > Switch to Perspective. Perspective makes nearby things look larger, like a photograph. Parallel keeps parallel edges parallel, which is useful when you want to judge sizes and alignment by eye.
