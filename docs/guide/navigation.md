# Moving around

You can build a whole model from one viewpoint, but it is much easier to turn the design around and look at it from several sides. These simple moves let you find the angle you need.

## Orbit

Orbit turns the camera around the model so you can see it from a different side. Press `O` to choose the Orbit tool, then drag with the left mouse button. You can also orbit at any time by dragging with the middle mouse button, which saves you from switching tools. Orbit a little, let go, and orbit again; short drags keep the view steady and make it easy to stop exactly where you want.

## Pan

Pan slides the whole view sideways without turning it, like moving a sheet of paper on a table. Press `H` for the Pan tool, then drag. If you are already orbiting, hold `Shift` while you drag with the middle button and the view will pan instead of turning. Panning is handy when your model is large and you want to look at a corner that has slid off the screen.

## Zoom

The scroll wheel zooms toward the cursor, so the spot you point at stays under your pointer as it grows or shrinks. Press `Z` for the Zoom tool and drag up and down to zoom in and out. If you get lost, press `Shift+Z` for Zoom Extents, which fits the whole model neatly in the window. Zoom Extents is a quick way to recover after a big change.

## Standard views

The standard views jump straight to a known direction, which is useful when you want to check that two pieces line up. Press `F2` for Iso, `F3` for Top, `F4` for Front, `F5` for Right, `F6` for Back, `F7` for Left and `F8` for Bottom. Moving between these views helps you spot a mistake that is hard to see from only one angle.

## Perspective and parallel

Press `Shift+P` to switch between perspective and parallel projection, or use the View menu. In perspective, near things look larger, just as they do to your eye. In parallel projection, edges that are parallel in the design stay parallel on screen, which is good for judging sizes and measuring by eye. Try both on the same model and notice how the shape reads differently.
